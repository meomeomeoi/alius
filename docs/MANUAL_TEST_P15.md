# Manual test — P15 the dynamic: hold, frames, domains, exit (in SillyTavern)

Run in a fresh campaign with four imported soldiers, at least two of them with a practice section on the card (a `practice` block with a primary style and a category such as `restraint`, and an `expectations` block) and a third without one. In SYS › Content turn on `restraint`, `worship`, `service` and `humiliation`. Turn **Debug mode** on (SYS › Debug lists every call to the mechanic model and its prompt; the main prompt, with its `dynamic:` lines, is in SillyTavern's prompt inspector).
The dynamic is slow and quiet: bands, words, cards and a few notices. Nothing here shows her hold, her Regard, what others suspect, the xp behind a mastery, what she keeps of the commander's before she has said it, or the stage of an aftermath, unless "show raw values" is on for the hold. Nothing gives the commander a pronoun or says what the commander feels.

1. **Import a card with practice and expectations, and lock a field.** In the calibration step each imported soldier has a PRACTICE block (style pair, before-others, inclination, fairness, how she invites, drive, and a line per category) and an EXPECTATIONS block (holds until, tells, worship by zone, build, hair, traits). Edit a field and press its lock; re-sync the card from the SQUAD tab and check the locked field is kept while the others take the card's new value. A soldier without a practice section has none of it and cannot start to hold.
2. **WORLD PARAMETERS › DYNAMIC.** With a practice category on, the block shows holders (0, 1, 2, 3, any), pace, frequency and, for each soldier, may hold (yes / no / auto), sensitivity, seeded and starting frame. Holders `0` turns the whole dynamic off. Seed one soldier with a starting frame; with a head start above zero, "the dynamic is already underway" is offered. Start the campaign: the seeded soldier's DYNAMIC section (step 3) already shows her frame and a band. With no practice category switched on, the block is not there and the campaign plays as before (step 14).
3. **Her dossier › DYNAMIC.** On the seeded soldier's dossier read the section: FRAME, HOLD as a band (the number in brackets only with SYS › Display › show raw values), DOMAINS she holds and "SHE MAY CLAIM", the tracks she leads as words, her reading in her own word once she has read something, and the exit button `[ END IT ]`. Every row can be examined with `?`. A soldier at `equal` who holds nothing has none of it; a soldier without a dynamic has no section.
4. **A probe in the prompt.** Open a scene with a soldier who practises and is not yet a holder. Within a few scenes the main prompt carries one `dynamic:` line for her: a deniable thing she tries (a small test of who leads), worded for the model and lasting one scene. Answer it in the scene (yield, deflect, resist or return it) and look at the extractor's call in SYS › Debug: it offers `probe_answered` for her and nothing for a soldier who has no probe. Her reading on the dossier appears after a few answers.
5. **`first_yes` in a scene.** Keep yielding to the same soldier's probes. When her hold is high enough she asks the first step in a scene; the prompt carries her request until it is answered. Say yes in the story (or press `[ ACCEPT ]` on her card in the scene or the thread). A toast in her voice and a cut-in say the frame moved; her dossier now reads PLAY. Refuse another step later and see the cost: a lower hold (about one band) and a cooldown before she asks again. The refusal is bookkeeping, not a memory fact (R24); she answers it through her directive.
6. **`first_protocol` as a booking.** The next step from play to role comes as a booking in her calendar: the hub's Tonight list names her and the schedule shows it. Keep the booking (or cancel it and see what that costs). Her frame becomes ROLE.
7. **A claim card.** Once her hold passes the band a domain needs, a claim card in her voice arrives in the scene, in her thread and on her dossier: the domain by its label, what it grants in plain words, and `[ YIELD ]` `[ REFUSE ]` `[ TALK ]`. YIELD gives her the domain (the CMDR card now says "Held by {her name}" with the domain); REFUSE costs by her frame; TALK opens a one-to-one scene with the claim still open. A second soldier asking for a domain the first holds settles between them without a card.
8. **A holder's override.** Once a holder has the `command` domain (the handover step), give an order (an activity, a roster, a decision): now and then an OVERRIDE modal opens in her voice. COMPLY follows her; DEFY goes ahead and she answers the way her fairness says, with nothing of the Leash's punishment. With a LEASHSWAP warden also set (MODE), the warden rolls first and only one override is ever open: a second roll waits for the first answer. Reload mid-override: it is still open and answers the same.
9. **The Leash block and a comms-terminal bypass.** With a holder who has the `leash_block` capability (the device domain), file a Leash request from the PDA on anyone: the request goes to her first and she signs, or holds it ("held the request and did not sign it", nothing is spent). Then use the comms room terminal (an OPS activity that costs the slot), the workshop reset with a technical ally, or another soldier's own channel: the request files, and she may find out. After a few held requests oversight's inquiry starts in the corporation's voice and a thread appears; it may end in a reprimand, a threat to move her (a line on the hub's Tonight list) or the controller used on her.
10. **The squad learns.** With others present let the commander wear a token of the dynamic or take a contact of it. The next scene with a witness carries a `dynamic:` line for what she has noticed (never what she knows exactly). Their reaction comes later, once they have shown it in a scene or a message; the dossier's WHO KNOWS row names them only then. A witness who dislikes it changes how she treats the commander.
11. **An exit with its aftermath.** On a holder's dossier press `[ END IT ]`. The card shows ABRUPT, TALK IT THROUGH, HAND OVER (only with another holder) and FORCE, each with its cost as a word (a vow reads "a break"). TALK IT THROUGH opens a scene and stays pending until a compromise is reached (or ABRUPT is pressed from the same card). FORCE is soft-disabled until a means has been done on her today: her DISCIPLINE menu (a vote may come first), the Leash, or the oversight request. Leave her: a notice in her voice and a cut-in, and the hub's Tonight list carries the notice of the stage her aftermath is in (never its name or its days). Her return offer, when it comes, is a card with ACCEPT and REFUSE.
12. **The offer composer and the body map.** In a scene with a soldier who practises, `[ OFFER MYSELF ]` opens the composer (a part, a posture, a contact, a service, time, a role, endurance; at most two). Offer something she craves: it lands; offer something she dislikes: it is a blunder, and she may correct it. Her dossier's body map (and its list) shows a grey `?` for every zone until a fact has shown it; the zone then takes her attitude and its mastery dots. Readable mode shows the list alone.
13. **Immersive mode.** SYS › Display › "Hide the dynamic's numbers": her DYNAMIC section and every band go, the frame chip in the scene goes, and the analysis panels answer "you feel it rather than read it". The cards in the scene and the thread, her notices, the toasts and the hub's Tonight line all still show. Turn it off and the section is back.
14. **A campaign with no practice category looks as before.** Start (or load) a campaign with no practice category on, or with holders `0`: no DYNAMIC block in the setup or the dossier, no card, no chip, no `dynamic:` line in any prompt, no new option in the extractor's prompt, and the same day to day as before this package. An old save loads without a migration.

Report anything that shows a number she keeps, a band in immersive mode, a card or a notice that is missing in immersive mode, a refusal that is not answered, a forced exit that goes through without its means, a second override open at once, a fact she keeps shown before she has said it, a mastery that shows its xp, or a pronoun for the commander.

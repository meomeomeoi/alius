# The debate and the briefing: her mental superiority (P16c, draft of 2026-11-28)

A draft from the design talk with the author (2026-11-28). The author edits here; the spec takes it over once the prototype rounds are done. Origin (author): sparring gave her a way to put the commander in place by the body; this is the other side, the mind. Above all `regal` proving her superiority ("you see? you are good for being my chair, and nothing else"): not the commander's own choice to yield when she probes, but **facts** where the commander loses to her.

## Decided so far (author)

- **C: both.** Her standing tally of facts from the campaign (her pick on an ASK HER card against the commander's, her plan against the commander's, P9e's her call) and her direct challenge, played as a mini-game.
- **Two games:** the **briefing** (memory: light, quick to build and tune) and the **debate** (the deepest and most replayable of all the mini-games).
- **A new soldier stat for the mind** (working name `intellect`), read only by these games for now. A commander of average mind still rarely beats a clever `regal` holder. A holder who is not clever avoids mind duels the further the commander is ahead of her (she proposes them less and accepts them less), and her **self-confidence** (a new field) can override that ("I'll lose to the commander" is not a thought she has).
- **The debate is typed** (no cards): the commander writes freely. The model never decides who is right; it answers structural questions the engine can check, and the engine keeps the score:
  - **anonymous and symmetric:** the classifier gets both lines as "A" and "B" in a random order and reads them by the same rubric (it cannot flatter the player: it does not know which one is the player);
  - **a move is proven by a quote:** to count as a rebuttal of her point the model must quote the commander's words and name the point; the engine checks the quote is in the text and shares her point's key words (the engine set them);
  - **the weak reading by default:** doubt, a failed call or a low-confidence answer reads as a weak point, never a strong one (P14's retry and reasons as for every call);
  - **her moves are the engine's:** the engine orders each of her lines with a quality (below); the model only words it;
  - **shown as read:** under the commander's line a short note ("read as: a rebuttal of 'equal rations feed the lazy'"); one challenge of a reading per debate, at the cost of a turn (to decide);
  - **checked on the player's model:** a fixed set of test lines (strong, weak, off-topic, personal attack, empty flattery, "I'm right because I'm right") run against the configured model in SYS, beside the other generation checks; the classifier is the mechanic model at a low temperature.
- **Lore:** a topic names its lore (`lore`: lorebook entries and registry ids); both her line's call and the classifier get it; a claim against the lore is a factual error (proven by a quote). Campaign facts only through `Knowledge` (nothing hidden from the commander).
- **Who wins is the floor, not the truth** (the same rules for every topic, philosophy included): a point left unanswered stands and scores, an answered one is neutralised; new reasons over repetition; contradicting oneself costs; factual errors cost (they matter little in philosophy). **With witnesses the room decides**, each by her own values (P1 value axes); alone, the score. Whether she admits a loss is her character: the engine knows who won; a `regal` may declare she won anyway, and the digest carries the truth ("she lost the argument, knows it, and will not admit it").
- **No knowledge areas in the profile** (author): `intellect` is general learning (a clever woman argues anything at "she studied it once" level, calculus included). A topic has a **depth** instead (`everyday`, `professional`, `academic`): a deep topic pulls a less clever woman's moves down, hardly touches a clever one. **Her own ground** is only a bonus, read from what the profile already has (her role, her hobbies' tags, her past by her card): one step up, no penalty without it. The same for the commander (the crime, P7 traits).

## The scale of the mind (author, 2026-11-28: decided: 1–10 and the abilities)

The author wants to feel a `regal` holder crush an average commander. Proposal:

- **`intellect` 1–10 for soldiers** (not 1–5): 3–6 ordinary, 7–8 brilliant, 9–10 a rare genius (the author may set it on her card; import proposes it from the card).
- **The commander's wits on the same scale:** `insight` + `cunning` (each 1–5), so 2–10; an average commander (3 + 3) is a 6.
- **Her line's quality by the gap** (her `intellect` − the commander's wits, ± the topic's depth and her ground): the shares of strong, fair and weak moves move with the gap; at a gap of +3 and more she also gets **abilities** a lesser mind does not have:
  - **pre-emption** (gap ≥ +2): she answers the commander's obvious next point before it is made (that point is worth less when it comes);
  - **the commander's words against the commander** (gap ≥ +3): she quotes an earlier line back and shows the contradiction;
  - **checkmate** (gap ≥ +4): once she leads by a margin she may end it with a closing point ("we're done here"), and the debate is over.
- **The commander's stats as help, the player's skill first:** `insight` gives a hint at her weak point ("her point rests on the assumption that…") and more time to answer; `cunning` a bonus for catching her contradictions; `authority` the witnesses' trust. The time to answer follows her `tempo`.

## The opening (author, 2026-11-28)

A debate never starts from nothing: when it begins, a first argument or question to argue with is already on the table, so the player does not have to invent what to argue against. At the least when she starts it: her opening statement on the motion (a claim and her reasons), worded by the model to the engine's order, its points' key words set by the engine. How it looks and what the commander's opening is when the commander starts: see the proposal in the talk (to be written here once decided).

## How a debate runs (author, 2026-11-28: agreed, with the author's four notes)

1. **The challenge:** a card with the motion, the framing, the stake and the commander's side. Refusing her challenge is itself a fact for her tally ("did not even dare").
2. **The opening** (see above): her opening statement written while the card was up (no wait for the model), or, when the commander starts, the motion, the commander's side and her position in one line.
3. **The board of points** (no numbers): two columns of short labels, hers and the commander's, each set by the engine from its key words. **Both sides rebut** (author's note 2), and **every point has a strength** (weak, fair, strong): hers by the engine's order; the commander's from the classifier's structural features (a new reason or a repeat, backed by an example or a fact, answering her strongest point, on the topic, true to the lore). A strong point needs a strong answer: a weak rebuttal only dents it. A point's states: **standing** (bright; a strong one bold), **dented**, **struck** (answered), **cracked** (caught in a contradiction). Whoever has more standing weight leads; the witnesses, if any, show the room's mood (nods, glances, murmurs).
4. **The exchanges:** her line, the commander's answer, against a time by her `tempo` (+ the commander's `insight`); missed, the turn is lost and her last point stands. **Their number varies** (author's note 1) with the topic's depth and how serious the debate is: everyday 3, professional 4, academic 5; +1 for a serious one (a stake, witnesses, a grievance); her `tempo` ±1. Her abilities (pre-emption, the commander's words against the commander, checkmate) act inside the exchanges.
5. **The end:** one short closing each, then the verdict: the room with witnesses (each by her values), the score alone; or early, by her checkmate or the commander's concession. Then her answer by style and outcome; whether she admits a loss is her character (the digest knows the truth); after her win, a finisher may follow, as after sparring.

## Without and with the dynamic (author's notes 3–4)

- **By default it is just a debate** between the commander and any named soldier, with no dynamic needed (later perhaps between two soldiers). Its consequences without the dynamic: relation deltas by the outcome and how it was argued (respect for a good argument, a sting for a humiliating one); **her opinions** (P14c: a debate on a topic that touches one of her opinions feeds that opinion's accumulator, so a commander who wins arguments can really change her mind, one step at a time, and a lost one can harden it); the witnesses' regard (P9b standing, outside perception); her tally of facts (memory).
- **With the dynamic: flavour by style** (`regal`: she is to be addressed properly while she argues, the `her_chair` framing; `cruel`: mockery; `ritual`: formal rules, the turns announced; `playful`: teasing; `cold`: few words) and **the start, the end and their weight tied to the dynamic**: her challenge has a motive as a bout does (`put_in_place`, `punishment`, `play`, `grievance`, `freedom`: the commander contradicted her in a scene, a probe of the commander's mind, her style's want to prove herself above the commander); after her win, hold, Regard, a finisher by the humiliation scale (ruling 11: "you see? good for being my chair" is a booking of the throne), the witnesses' fall in regard by what they saw; after the commander's win, her answer by style (an affront remembered, a grudge, or respect: Regard up), and a commander who keeps winning becomes a motive of hers.

## The vibe (author, 2026-11-28)

The commander and the player fight, try, try again, and someone is simply better; what is left is to come to terms with that fact over time, and she keeps pushing it in the commander's face. So: losses should feel close and earned by her, not by luck (the near miss is shown: "two of your points still stood at the end"); her abilities make it plain she is ahead ("I knew you would say that"); she counts and quotes (her tally, the formula); and accepting it is a reading of hers (a commander who keeps fighting and a commander who has come to accept it read differently, P15's motives).

**Growth (author, 2026-11-28: A, for now):** the commander's mind hardly grows. Practice (P16b's `speech` discipline, which has no consumer yet) gives only small things: more time to answer, hints at her weak point; the commander's wits on the scale do not rise, the gap to a clever holder stays, and only brilliant play wins.

## Starts and ends chosen (author, 2026-11-28; the list of 16 ideas is in the talk)

**Starts:**
- **"You said it" (works without the dynamic).** The commander states an opinion in a scene; the scene's extraction notes it (a new event, the commander's opinion as the extractor's short paraphrase, never the raw text); a day or two later she brings it back as the motion: "You said fear works better than love. Defend it." It ties to her own opinions (P14c).
- **From the throne (the dynamic).** The commander is her chair and she is bored: "Entertain me. Tell me why…"; the debate runs while the weight game goes on. **The throne's intensity is lowered for it** (author: else the player only corrects the balance and never answers): her shifts rarer and softer, no mug, no signing, no rising; while the commander types she does not shift (only the slow wander); losing the level during the commander's turn dents one of the commander's own points (the chair wobbled while it spoke), it does not lose the debate.
- **The commander challenges, on her terms (the dynamic).** She accepts only on her terms: she picks her side, the commander argues kneeling, and every point opens with her form ("with respect, …"); a missing form costs (a rule of conduct, as in the strokes).

**Ends:**
- **The formula.** The loser types it aloud, exactly: for `regal` her own words ("You were right, and I was wrong", "I'm good for being your chair"); refusing is defiance; what was said becomes her fact, quoted later ("you said it yourself").
- **The sentence from the motion (only where the motion allows it).** Winning, she draws the consequence from the debate itself ("The strong should rule the weak." → "So kneel."): a topic may carry the consequence it allows (a field on the topic; none: the usual finisher).
- **"Then you'll be my chair."** The throne at once, in the same scene; her evening goes on on the commander, now and then the lost point recalled ("Comfortable? No? You lost.").
- **The tally.** She keeps the count of debates; at round numbers she reads it out ("Seven to one. Shall we stop pretending?") and offers the next step of the dynamic.

## The briefing (the light game; its round 1 to build)

She reads a sequence once, in her hand, one item at a time (call signs with grid squares: "Kestrel 4-7", "Ash 2-9"; later a duty roster "name: post" or a code phrase); then she recites first (bored, flawless while her span holds), then the commander assembles it from tiles among decoys (memory, not typing). Each round is one longer (from 4); a round where one misses and the other does not decides; `regal` and `cruel` may add "and now backwards". Her span ≈ her `intellect` on the 1–10 scale + 3 (to tune); the commander's display time grows with `insight`, the decoys fewer with `cunning`; her chatter while she reads follows her `voice`. An average person's span is about 7.

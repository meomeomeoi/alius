# The debate and the briefing: her mental superiority (P16c, draft of 2026-11-28)

A draft from the design talk with the author (2026-11-28). The author edits here; the spec takes it over once the prototype rounds are done. Origin (author): sparring gave her a way to put the commander in place by the body; this is the other side, the mind. Above all `regal` proving her superiority ("you see? you are good for being my chair, and nothing else"): not the commander's own choice to yield when she probes, but **facts** where the commander loses to her.

## Decided so far (author)

- **C: both.** Her standing tally of facts from the campaign (her pick on an ASK HER card against the commander's, her plan against the commander's, P9e's her call) and her direct challenge, played as a mini-game.
- **Two games:** the **briefing** (memory: light, quick to build and tune) and the **debate** (the deepest and most replayable of all the mini-games).
- **A new soldier stat for the mind** (working name `intellect`), read only by these games for now. A commander of average mind still rarely beats a clever `regal` holder. A holder who is not clever avoids mind duels the further the commander is ahead of her (she proposes them less and accepts them less), and her **self-confidence** (a new field) can override that ("I'll lose to the commander" is not a thought she has).
- **The debate is typed** (no cards): the commander writes freely. The model never decides who is right; it answers structural questions the engine can check, and the engine keeps the score:
  - **anonymous and symmetric:** the classifier gets both lines as "A" and "B" in a random order and reads them by the same rubric (it cannot flatter the player: it does not know which one is the player);
  - **a move is proven by a quote:** to count as a rebuttal of her point the model must quote the commander's words and name the point; the engine checks the quote is in the text and shares her point's key words (the engine set them);
  - **the weak reading by default:** doubt, a failed call or a low-confidence answer reads as a weak point, never a strong one (P14's retry and reasons as for every call);
  - **her moves are the engine's:** the engine orders each of her lines with a quality (below); the model only words it;
  - **shown as read:** under the commander's line a short note ("read as: a rebuttal of 'equal rations feed the lazy'"); one challenge of a reading per debate, at the cost of a turn (to decide);
  - **checked on the player's model:** a fixed set of test lines (strong, weak, off-topic, personal attack, empty flattery, "I'm right because I'm right") run against the configured model in SYS, beside the other generation checks; the classifier is the mechanic model at a low temperature.
- **Lore:** a topic names its lore (`lore`: lorebook entries and registry ids); both her line's call and the classifier get it; a claim against the lore is a factual error (proven by a quote). Campaign facts only through `Knowledge` (nothing hidden from the commander).
- **Who wins is the floor, not the truth** (the same rules for every topic, philosophy included): a point left unanswered stands and scores, an answered one is neutralised; new reasons over repetition; contradicting oneself costs; factual errors cost (they matter little in philosophy). **With witnesses the room decides**, each by her own values (P1 value axes); alone, the score. Whether she admits a loss is her character: the engine knows who won; a `regal` may declare she won anyway, and the digest carries the truth ("she lost the argument, knows it, and will not admit it").
- **No knowledge areas in the profile** (author): `intellect` is general learning (a clever woman argues anything at "she studied it once" level, calculus included). A topic has a **depth** instead (`everyday`, `professional`, `academic`): a deep topic pulls a less clever woman's moves down, hardly touches a clever one. **Her own ground** is only a bonus, read from what the profile already has (her role, her hobbies' tags, her past by her card): one step up, no penalty without it. The same for the commander (the crime, P7 traits).

## Proposed: the scale of the mind (to confirm)

The author wants to feel a `regal` holder crush an average commander. Proposal:

- **`intellect` 1–10 for soldiers** (not 1–5): 3–6 ordinary, 7–8 brilliant, 9–10 a rare genius (the author may set it on her card; import proposes it from the card).
- **The commander's wits on the same scale:** `insight` + `cunning` (each 1–5), so 2–10; an average commander (3 + 3) is a 6.
- **Her line's quality by the gap** (her `intellect` − the commander's wits, ± the topic's depth and her ground): the shares of strong, fair and weak moves move with the gap; at a gap of +3 and more she also gets **abilities** a lesser mind does not have:
  - **pre-emption** (gap ≥ +2): she answers the commander's obvious next point before it is made (that point is worth less when it comes);
  - **the commander's words against the commander** (gap ≥ +3): she quotes an earlier line back and shows the contradiction;
  - **checkmate** (gap ≥ +4): once she leads by a margin she may end it with a closing point ("we're done here"), and the debate is over.
- **The commander's stats as help, the player's skill first:** `insight` gives a hint at her weak point ("her point rests on the assumption that…") and more time to answer; `cunning` a bonus for catching her contradictions; `authority` the witnesses' trust. The time to answer follows her `tempo`.

## The briefing (the light game; its round 1 to build)

She reads a sequence once, in her hand, one item at a time (call signs with grid squares: "Kestrel 4-7", "Ash 2-9"; later a duty roster "name: post" or a code phrase); then she recites first (bored, flawless while her span holds), then the commander assembles it from tiles among decoys (memory, not typing). Each round is one longer (from 4); a round where one misses and the other does not decides; `regal` and `cruel` may add "and now backwards". Her span ≈ her `intellect` on the 1–10 scale + 3 (to tune); the commander's display time grows with `insight`, the decoys fewer with `cunning`; her chatter while she reads follows her `voice`. An average person's span is about 7.

## Open (to decide with the author)

- Her challenge and the stake (as the sparring wager), her finishers after a won debate (ruling 11's scale), the digest, the memory check (a debate lost before witnesses is P15 §8's witness path; her tally of facts is her fact).
- The tally of facts from the campaign (C's other half): what she counts and how she uses it.
